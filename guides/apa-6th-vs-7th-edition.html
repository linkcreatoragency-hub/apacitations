APA 6th vs 7th Edition: What Changed?

By the APACitations Editorial Team · Updated August 2026

The 7th edition of the APA Publication Manual arrived in October 2019 and is now the standard everywhere — but plenty of older guides, templates, and even instructors still circulate APA 6 rules. If your citations "look right" but keep getting marked down, an outdated rule is probably why. Here is every change that matters.

Changes to in-text citations

The biggest one: et al. from the first citation. APA 6 made you list up to five authors on first mention (Rosenzweig, Breedlove, & Watson, 2005) and abbreviate only afterwards. APA 7 cuts straight to (Rosenzweig et al., 2005) for any source with three or more authors.

Language and writing changes

  • Singular "they" is officially endorsed both for people who use it and for generic references.
  • Bias-free language guidelines were expanded (age, disability, gender, race, socioeconomic status).
  • One space after a period, not two.

Paper format changes

  • A separate student title page was introduced — no running head required for student papers.
  • More font freedom: Calibri 11, Arial 11, Lucida Sans 10, Times New Roman 12, or Georgia 11.
  • New heading formats for levels 3–5 (all headings are now title case and bold).

Which edition should you use in 2026?

APA 7 — unless your instructor explicitly requires APA 6 (rare now, but it happens with older course templates). Our citation generator supports both: just pick the edition in the style dropdown and the formatting adjusts automatically.

Updating an old paper from APA 6 to APA 7

Converting an existing reference list is mostly deletion: remove publisher cities, remove "Retrieved from" before URLs, convert "doi:" prefixes to https://doi.org/ links, and delete database names. Then check in-text citations for three-or-more author sources and shorten them to et al. Finally, if it's a student paper, you can drop the running head. Frequently asked questions

Is APA 6 still accepted in 2026?
Only if your instructor or journal explicitly requires it — APA 7 has been the standard since late 2019. When no edition is specified, use APA 7.
Did in-text citation format change for one or two authors?
No. One- and two-author citations are identical in both editions: (Kahneman, 2011) and (Rasch & Born, 2013). Only the three-plus author rule changed.
Is there an APA 8th edition?
No. As of 2026 the 7th edition (2020) remains the current version of the Publication Manual, and no 8th edition has been announced.
