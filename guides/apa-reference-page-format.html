APA Reference Page Format

By the APACitations Editorial Team · Updated August 2026

The reference page is where markers look first — and where easy points are lost. The good news: APA reference page formatting follows a handful of mechanical rules. Get these right and your list will look professional every time.

Page setup rules

  • New page after the end of your text, before any tables or appendices.
  • Heading: the single word References, centered and bold, at the top. (Not "Bibliography" or "Works Cited".)
  • Double-spacing throughout — within and between entries, no extra blank lines.
  • Hanging indent: first line of each entry flush left, every following line indented 0.5 inch (1.27 cm).
  • Same font as the rest of the paper (e.g., 12 pt Times New Roman or 11 pt Calibri).

In Word or Google Docs, select the entries and choose Paragraph → Indentation → Special → Hanging. Never build the indent with spaces or tabs.

Alphabetical order rules

  • Sort by the first author's last name, letter by letter. "Nothing precedes something": Brown, J. comes before Browning, A.
  • No author? Alphabetize by the first significant word of the title (ignore A, An, The).
  • Same first author, different co-authors: the shorter entry comes first, then alphabetize by the second author.
  • Same author(s), different years: earliest year first. Same author, same year: alphabetize by title and add letters (2020a, 2020b).

What belongs on the reference page

Every source cited in your text — and only sources cited in your text. Two exceptions never get reference entries: personal communications (interviews, emails — cited in text only) and whole websites mentioned in passing.

Sample reference list

American Psychological Association. (2020). Publication manual of the American Psychological Association (7th ed.).
Kahneman, D. (2011). Thinking, fast and slow. Farrar, Straus and Giroux.
Kurzgesagt – In a Nutshell. (2021, August 12). How the immune system actually works [Video]. YouTube. https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=lXfEK8G8CUI
Rasch, B., & Born, J. (2013). About sleep's role in memory. Physiological Reviews, 93(2), 681–766. https://doi.org/10.1152/physrev.00032.2012
World Health Organization. (2022). Global status report on physical activity 2022. https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789240059153

Final checklist

  • ☐ Every in-text citation has a matching reference (and vice versa)
  • ☐ "References" heading — bold, centered
  • ☐ Alphabetical order, hanging indents, double-spaced
  • ☐ Italics on book titles, journal names, and volume numbers
  • ☐ Sentence case for article/book titles; Title Case for journal names
  • ☐ DOIs formatted as https://doi.org/... links

Setting up the page in Word and Google Docs

In Microsoft Word: select your references → Home → Paragraph dialog → Special: Hanging → 0.5". Set Line spacing to Double and remove Space After Paragraph. In Google Docs: Format → Align & indent → Indentation options → Special: Hanging → 0.5. Both editors keep the setting as you add entries, so format once and keep pasting citations from the generator.

References vs. Bibliography vs. Works Cited

The names are style-specific, not interchangeable: APA uses References (only cited sources), Chicago uses Bibliography (may include background reading), MLA uses Works Cited. Handing in an APA paper titled "Bibliography" is a small error that graders notice immediately.

Frequently asked questions

Do references count toward my word limit?
Usually not — most universities exclude the reference list from word counts — but policies differ, so check your assignment brief or ask your instructor.
How do I alphabetize names with prefixes like van or de?
Treat the prefix as part of the surname as the author writes it: van Dijk files under V, De Silva under D. Ignore capitalization when sorting.
What if I cited something but can't find the full details?
Track the source down — a reference needs author, date, title, and source. If a detail truly doesn't exist (no date, no author) APA has rules for each gap; missing details you simply couldn't find are a research problem, not a formatting one.
