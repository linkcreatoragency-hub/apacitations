What is an APA citation?

An APA citation credits the sources you used in your work, following the style rules of the American Psychological Association. APA style is the standard in psychology, education, nursing, business, and most social sciences. It has two parts that work together:

1. The in-text citation

A brief marker in the body of your paper showing where information came from. APA uses the author–date system: the author's last name and the year of publication, e.g. (Kahneman, 2011) — or with a page number for direct quotes: (Kahneman, 2011, p. 24).

2. The reference list entry

The full publication details, listed alphabetically on a "References" page at the end of your paper. Each entry uses a hanging indent and contains four elements: author, date, title, and source. The generator above builds both parts for you automatically.

Why citing matters

Correct citations protect you from plagiarism, let readers verify your sources, and show you have researched the topic properly. Most universities deduct marks for incorrect referencing — so a reliable citation machine saves both time and grades. How do I cite a website in APA format?
The basic format is: Author, A. A. (Year, Month Day). Title of page. Site Name. URL. If there's no author, start with the title; if there's no date, use (n.d.). Paste any URL into the generator above and it extracts these details automatically. Full rules in our website citation guide.
What's the difference between a reference and an in-text citation?
The in-text citation is the short marker inside your paper — (Smith, 2020) — while the reference is the full entry on the References page at the end. Every in-text citation must have a matching reference list entry.
