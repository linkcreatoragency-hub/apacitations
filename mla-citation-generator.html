What is MLA format?

MLA format is the citation style of the Modern Language Association, used mainly in English, literature, and other humanities courses. Unlike APA's author–date system, MLA uses author–page in-text citations — for example (Morrison 27) — and a "Works Cited" page listing every source in full.

MLA 9 (the current, 2021 edition) builds every entry from a universal list of "core elements": author, title of source, title of container, other contributors, version, number, publisher, publication date, and location. Website

Journal article

MLA vs APA — which one do I need?

Use MLA for literature, languages, and humanities papers; use APA for psychology, education, and social sciences. If you're not sure, check your assignment brief or ask your instructor — and read our full APA vs MLA comparison. You can switch styles any time with the dropdown above.

Frequently asked questions

What is an MLA citation generator?
A free tool that formats your sources into correct MLA 9th edition Works Cited entries automatically. Enter a URL, ISBN, DOI, or the details manually, and it produces the citation with correct punctuation and italics.
What does an MLA in-text citation look like?
MLA uses the author's last name and a page number with no comma: (Morrison 27). If there is no author, use a shortened title. If there is no page number, the author's name alone is enough.
Do MLA citations include the URL?
Yes. MLA 9 includes the URL (without https://) as the location for online sources. An access date is optional but recommended when the source has no publication date.
