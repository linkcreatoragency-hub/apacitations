APA In-Text Citations: The Complete Guide

By the APACitations Editorial Team · Updated August 2026

Every source you use in an APA paper appears twice: once in the reference list, and once (or more) as an in-text citation in your writing. APA's author–date system is simple at its core — name and year — but the details matter. This guide covers every rule you'll actually need.

Parenthetical vs narrative citations

There are two equally correct ways to cite in text:

Parenthetical: Sleep plays a central role in memory consolidation (Rasch & Born, 2013).
Narrative: Rasch and Born (2013) showed that sleep plays a central role in memory consolidation.

Note the connector changes: "&" inside parentheses, but the word "and" in running text.

When to add page numbers

Page numbers are required for direct quotes and encouraged for paraphrases of specific passages:

"Memory consolidation benefits primarily from slow-wave sleep" (Rasch & Born, 2013, p. 690).

Use "p." for one page, "pp." for a range (pp. 690–692). For sources without pages, use a paragraph number (para. 4), a section heading, or a timestamp for video (2:45).

The et al. rule

Number of authorsFirst citationLater citations
1–2(Rasch & Born, 2013)Same every time
3 or more(Rosenzweig et al., 2005)(Rosenzweig et al., 2005)

APA 7 simplified this: three or more authors are shortened to "et al." from the very first citation (APA 6 required listing up to five authors the first time).

Special cases

No author

Use the first words of the title — in quotation marks for articles and pages, italicized for books: ("The History of Citation," 2022).

No date

Use n.d.: (Smith, n.d.).

Organization as author

Spell it out the first time with the abbreviation in brackets, then abbreviate: first (World Health Organization [WHO], 2023), then (WHO, 2023).

Multiple sources in one citation

Alphabetical order, separated by semicolons: (Kahneman, 2011; Rasch & Born, 2013).

Same author, same year

Add letters to the year, matching the reference list: (Smith, 2020a, 2020b).

Secondary sources (as cited in)

If you read about Piaget's study in Kahneman's book, cite: (Piaget, 1936, as cited in Kahneman, 2011) — and only Kahneman appears in your reference list. Use this sparingly; find the original when you can.

Common mistakes

  • Putting the citation after the final period — it belongs inside the sentence: "…consolidation (Rasch & Born, 2013)."
  • Including the author's first name or initials in text citations.
  • Citing a source in text that's missing from the reference list (or vice versa) — they must match one-to-one.
  • Using "et al." for two authors — always name both.
