How to Cite a Website in APA Format

By the APACitations Editorial Team · Updated August 2026

Websites are the most commonly cited source type in student papers — and also the one with the most "what if" situations: no author, no date, an organization instead of a person. This guide gives you the exact APA 7th edition format for every case, with copy-ready examples.

The basic APA format for a website

An APA 7 reference for a web page has four building blocks — author, date, title, and source:

Author, A. A. (Year, Month Day). Title of page. Site Name. https://www.url.com

Note three details that trip students up: the date includes the month and day when available, the page title is italicized and written in sentence case (only the first word, proper nouns, and the first word after a colon are capitalized), and the URL is given in full with no "Retrieved from" label and no final period.

Example
Chowdhury, R. (2024, March 12). The health benefits of walking every day. Healthline. https://www.healthline.com/health/benefits-of-walking

Website with no author

If no individual author is named, check whether an organization is responsible for the content — if so, the organization is the author:

World Health Organization. (2023, June 5). Physical activity fact sheet. https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/physical-activity

When the author and the site name are the same (as above), APA says to omit the site name to avoid repetition. If there is truly no author at all, move the title into the author position:

The history of citation styles. (2022, January 8). Writing Hub. https://www.writinghub.com/citation-history

Website with no date

Use (n.d.) — "no date" — in place of the year. If the content is likely to change over time (like a frequently updated statistics page), add a retrieval date:

Smith, J. (n.d.). Guide to healthy eating. Nutrition Base. Retrieved August 7, 2026, from https://www.nutritionbase.com/guide

In-text citations for websites

The in-text citation is always the author (or organization, or short title) plus the year:

SituationParentheticalNarrative
Author known(Chowdhury, 2024)Chowdhury (2024) explains…
Organization as author(World Health Organization, 2023)The World Health Organization (2023)…
No author("The History of Citation," 2022)—
No date(Smith, n.d.)Smith (n.d.) suggests…

Websites rarely have page numbers; for a direct quote, use a paragraph number or section heading instead: (Chowdhury, 2024, para. 4).

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Citing the whole website instead of the page. APA cites the specific page you used, with its own title and URL. A whole website is only mentioned in text, not in the reference list.
  • Adding "Retrieved from" before every URL. APA 7 dropped this — only use "Retrieved [date], from" when a retrieval date is needed.
  • Using title case for page titles. APA uses sentence case: The health benefits of walking, not The Health Benefits of Walking.
  • Forgetting (n.d.). Never leave the date position empty.
