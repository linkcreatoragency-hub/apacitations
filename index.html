What is an APA citation?

An APA citation credits the sources you used in your work, following the style rules of the American Psychological Association. APA style is the standard in psychology, education, nursing, business, and most social sciences. It has two parts that work together:

1. The in-text citation

A brief marker in the body of your paper showing where information came from. APA uses the author–date system: the author's last name and the year of publication, e.g. (Kahneman, 2011) — or with a page number for direct quotes: (Kahneman, 2011, p. 24).

2. The reference list entry

The full publication details, listed alphabetically on a "References" page at the end of your paper. Each entry uses a hanging indent and contains four elements: author, date, title, and source. The generator above builds both parts for you automatically.

Why citing matters

Correct citations protect you from plagiarism, let readers verify your sources, and show you have researched the topic properly. Most universities deduct marks for incorrect referencing — so a reliable citation machine saves both time and grades. APA format quick rules (7th edition)

Citations are only half of APA style — your paper itself follows formatting rules too. Here is a cheat sheet of the rules students look up most often:

ElementAPA 7 rule
FontTimes New Roman 12, Calibri 11, Arial 11, Lucida Sans Unicode 10, or Georgia 11
SpacingDouble-spaced throughout, including the reference list
Margins1 inch (2.54 cm) on all sides
In-text citation(Author, Year) — add p. or para. for direct quotes
3+ authorsFirst author + "et al." from the very first citation
Reference pageTitled "References", alphabetical, hanging indent 0.5"
Titles in referencesSentence case; italicize books, journals & standalone works
DOI / URLFull link (https://doi.org/…), no "Retrieved from", no final period

Who uses APA style?

APA is the most widely required citation style at universities worldwide. If you study psychology, education, nursing, social work, business, economics, criminology, or communication, chances are your papers must follow APA rules. Many scientific journals in these fields also require APA-formatted manuscripts, so mastering it early pays off far beyond your first essay.

Because APA's author–date system shows readers how recent your evidence is at a glance, it suits fields where currency of research matters. That's also why instructors check citations carefully — a clean, consistent reference list signals careful work. Our generator applies the current 7th edition rules automatically, and every reference comes with its matching in-text citation so the two always agree.

Tips for a perfect reference list

Collect your sources as you research rather than at the end — paste each URL, ISBN, or DOI into the generator and copy the result into a working document. Alphabetize once at the end (our reference page guide explains the ordering rules), apply a hanging indent in Word or Google Docs, and give everything one final read against your assignment brief. Five minutes of checking protects marks that took weeks to earn.

Frequently asked questions

What is an APA citation generator?

How do I cite a website in APA format?
The basic format is: Author, A. A. (Year, Month Day). Title of page. Site Name. URL. If there's no author, start with the title; if there's no date, use (n.d.). Paste any URL into the generator above and it extracts these details automatically. Full rules in our website citation guide.
What's the difference between a reference and an in-text citation?
The in-text citation is the short marker inside your paper — (Smith, 2020) — while the reference is the full entry on the References page at the end. Every in-text citation must have a matching reference list entry.
Does this tool support APA 6th edition?
Yes. The default is APA 7th edition (current since 2020), but the style dropdown also offers APA 6, MLA 9, Chicago 17, and Harvard. See what changed between APA 6 and 7.
Are the citations accurate enough for university work?
The generator follows the official APA Publication Manual (7th edition) formats for the supported source types. As with any citation tool, we recommend a quick final check against your institution's style guide, since some universities apply small local variations.
How do I cite a source with no author?
Move the title into the author position of the reference, and use a shortened title in the in-text citation: ("The History of Citation," 2022). If an organization is responsible for the content, use the organization as the author instead. Leave the author fields empty in the generator and it handles this automatically.
How do I cite a source with no date?
Use (n.d.) — "no date" — in place of the year, both in the reference and the in-text citation: (Smith, n.d.). Leave the year field empty in the generator and it inserts n.d. for you.
What is a hanging indent and how do I make one?
Every reference's first line sits flush left while the following lines are indented 0.5 inch. In Word or Google Docs, select your references and choose Format → Paragraph → Indentation → Special → Hanging. Never build the indent with spaces or tabs.
Do I need to cite every sentence?
No — cite when you use someone else's ideas, data, or words. A paragraph drawing on one source can often carry a single citation placed where the borrowed material appears, as long as it's clear which claims come from the source. Direct quotes always need a citation with a page number or paragraph locator.
